## Asset truth and compatibility

The main route selects full Human, Orc and **`equipment-undead`**, plus the compressed startup Human pack. The old `equipment-undead-provisional` pack is historical; existing `test-undead-race.mjs` chiefly exercises that older pack and therefore does not establish active-pack bind compatibility. The inspector follows the live pack path in `main.js`, reads 36 manifest entries through glTF Transform's installed `NodeIO`/Meshopt support, validates encoded/decoded hashes and fit versions, and reports geometry, textures, material names, joint hierarchy/order/rest/bind, mesh frame, finite normalized skin weights, animations and semantic accessor hashes. It emits rows before failure and exits **1** for the eight active structural incompatibilities.

| Family | Body and garment result | Reuse implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human full | 65 joints, 57 clips; all 8 garment entries match the body rest hierarchy and inverse binds. Body: 5,325 triangles. | One measured fit/bind family, suitable for M002 descriptor and an M003 single-family trial. |
| Orc | 65 joints, 57 clips; all 8 garment entries match its body. Body: 34,268 triangles. | Its own fit/shape family; does not batch with Human solely because joint names match. |
| Undead active | Body has 65 joints/57 clips; **all 8 garment entries disagree with its body at every joint rest/inverse bind**. Largest rest-matrix component delta is ~0.154; largest inverse-bind component delta ~15.425 in source units. Body: 8,981 triangles. | Do not classify the active dressed Undead as deformation compatible or include it in an exact shared-pose crowd group until its garments are rebound and revalidated. The current runtime accepts the declared fit and shares the body palette; this census exposes the gap. |
| Human startup | Gzip entries decode to the declared hashes; startup body has identical bind, geometry semantic hash and animation semantic hashes to full Human, while the full file hash differs due to the startup texture/packaging. | Startup/full identity is semantic; a different GLB hash alone is not a fit change. |

Joint-index mapping is explicit in each compatibility row. The current loader borrows the body palette without rewriting `JOINTS_0`, so a reordered garment is **incompatible at runtime** even when the calculated mapping is bijective; it needs an offline remap first. Identity mesh-frame checks and 1e-5 matrix tolerance allow float serialization noise; weight sums use the existing streamed-asset test's 1e-3 threshold. The inspector does not silently treat missing skin, malformed weights, missing entries or fit-version disagreement as valid.

The catalogue declares eight skinned garment entries and four procedural hand props (`sword`, `staff`, `greatstaff`, `book`) across seven slots; props have no GLB skin to batch. Coverage is authored in Human region names and resolved by `RACE_BODY_ALIASES` in `equipment-stream.js`. The Undead hood's `HumanHair` mask has no Undead body mesh to hide, a deliberate no-op. A declaration is not evidence that a particular body mesh is actually hidden; Human's single `HumanV1Body` and Undead's `UndeadV1Body` are not the six separate Orc-style body regions. M003 must test real dressed combinations and material/skin layout, not infer groups from fit IDs or names alone.

Runtime ownership: each garment keeps its own geometry, material and skin buffers. `equipment-stream.js` temporarily borrows the live body skeleton's `boneTexture` and `boneMatrices`, restores the garment's owned skeleton before removing the container, and disposes loaded entries through the equipment loader. A future crowd pool must keep the body palette alive while borrowers exist and preserve this disposal order. The old `src/character/runtime/body-profile.js` MakeHuman/diagnostic profile is not the active main-route body.

## Fresh performance baseline

M1 Max / macOS 26.6.2 on AC power. Built Pages-mode bundle on the compressed local preview; Chromium WebGPU headless with uncapped flags; actual 1280×720 buffer, seven enemies, Havok active, no recording. Five routes, three 12-second runs each. Values below are per-route mean of run mean FPS; p95 range shows all runs, and worst is the largest single saved `requestAnimationFrame` interval. Nearest-rank p50/p95/p99 and counts above 6.94/8.33/16.67/33.33 ms are stored per run next to **all raw intervals** in `region-fps.json`. They are presentation intervals, not CPU-only or GPU timestamp duration; GPU mean is separate in the underlying engine metrics.

| Route | Mean FPS | Run p95 range (ms) | Worst (ms) | Total frames >8.33 ms |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Meadow | 190.5 | 6.3–6.4 | 11.6 | 15 |
| Town | 192.1 | 6.0–6.1 | 10.9 | 6 |
| Bridge | 211.5 | 5.8–10.2 | 14.1 | 583 |
| Cathedral | 219.5 | 5.4–10.2 | 12.8 | 761 |
| Forest | 211.4 | 5.5–9.9 | 11.3 | 504 |

All route means exceed 144 FPS and the historical >120 floor. Bridge run 3, cathedral runs 1–2 and forest run 2 show intermittent pacing with 8.33 ms exceedances despite high throughput; this baseline does not claim every frame meets a 144 FPS budget. The worst 14.1 ms exceeds the released 12.3 ms sampled worst, while meadow/town means are a few FPS below the released 194–231 range. The runs are not a same-session paired A/B and changed no runtime code, so this is an inherited observation, not a demonstrated code regression. M003 should pair candidate and baseline runs in the same isolation conditions before attributing a >5% tail change.

Cold startup used 20 fresh **headless** Chrome processes at emulated 50 Mbit/s / 40 ms with the same built preview. All reached grounded, dressed, GPU-completed playable state and responded to movement; no console/GPU errors were reported. Median **791.1 ms**, nearest-rank p95 **802.1 ms**, **19/20 <=1 second**, maximum **9,149.4 ms**. In the first process, the first GPU-completed frame arrived at 9,134.2 ms after a supported-frame submission at 1,899.3 ms; that outlier is retained. Browser profiles/cache were fresh, while operating-system and GPU-driver caches were not controlled. This is not a universal one-second guarantee. The earlier production 50/40 result had p95 979.4 ms and max 1,054.5 ms under its own conditions.

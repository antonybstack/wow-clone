# M005 result — body and garment deformation proof

Status: **implemented, measured and visually reviewed, with two rejected approaches and the residual exceptions named** (2026-09-27 local time). Source base: `99bb5ef` (M004) plus the M005 working tree; the pre-existing `AGENTS.md` edit was preserved. The [brief](../m005-deformation-proof.md) defines the gate. No shipped character, no production asset, no catalogue entry and no deployment changed.

## What was built

[`girth-field.mjs`](../../../../scripts/character-assets/girth-field.mjs) carries the M004 girth field plus the two fitting rules M005 adds, and is what the garment builder uses. `build-human-shape-family.mjs` still holds its own copy of the field: a refactor onto the shared module was attempted and verified to reproduce the body candidate's hash byte for byte, but it was reverted rather than landed half-finished, so the two implementations are duplicated today and should be reconciled before either changes. All eight Human garments are skinned to the same 65-joint rest skeleton in the same metre space as the body, so a field defined in bone space applies to all of them without a per-garment authoring pass. [`build-garment-shape-family.mjs`](../../../../scripts/character-assets/build-garment-shape-family.mjs) gives each garment the same two targets the body has, in the same order, so **one weight vector drives the whole outfit**; each piece's neutral identity is hashed against the shipped garment over its attributes, indices, joint order and inverse binds, and the base is unchanged.

The catalogue has no rigid element, so M005 authored one: [`build_warden_pauldrons.py`](../../../../scripts/character-assets/build_warden_pauldrons.py) derives a pair of shoulder pauldrons from the shipped body's own deltoid surface in an isolated Blender 5.2.1 session, floats them 32 mm off the skin, solidifies them into a 10 mm shell, and weights **every vertex 1.0 to a single bone**. Source project: [`blender/characters/warden-pauldrons-v1.blend`](../../../../blender/characters/warden-pauldrons-v1.blend). It is a prototype: crude, slightly asymmetric — 122 and 138 vertices per side, because the body it is cut from is not perfectly symmetric — and deliberately absent from `public/` and from the production catalogue.

## The fitting rule, and the two that were rejected

The measurement below is the same in all three rows: for each garment, how many body vertices the shipped configuration covers and this shape does not. Neutral is the control and scores 0 by construction, so anything reported is something the shape broke.

| Garment and shape | Shipped garments | Girth field applied to the garment | Reference fit tracking the body |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Tunic, slender | 43 | 4 | **0** |
| Tunic, stout | 364 | 41 | **17** |
| Trousers, slender | 11 | 24 | **2** |
| Trousers, stout | 250 | 27 | **14** |
| Boots, slender | 0 | 0 | **0** |
| Boots, stout | 9 | 3 | **0** |
| **Total** | **677** | **99** | **33** |

Applying the girth field to a garment's own offsets scales that vertex's distance from its bone axis, and a garment sits at a larger radius than the skin beneath it. Stout therefore pushes cloth further out than the body moves, which is harmless; slender pulls it in further than the body recedes, which drives cloth *into* the skin — the trousers went from 11 newly uncovered vertices to 24, worse than doing nothing. Tracking instead moves each garment vertex by the inverse-distance-weighted displacement of the eight nearest body vertices, so the authored standoff is preserved in both directions. That is the shipped rule; the field mode is retained behind `ASHEN_FIT_MODE=field` so the comparison can be rerun.

**The 33 that remain** are not noise. The worst tunic case is a body vertex 42.4 mm from the cloth at the hem behind the hip, and the worst trousers case is 53.8 mm at the outer mid-thigh. Both are hem boundaries, where a heavier silhouette extends past where the garment ends. A reference fit preserves standoff; it does not add material a bigger body needs. That is an authoring job, not a fitting one, and it is the honest exception this milestone reports rather than tuning the metric until it disappears.

## The plate: two wrong answers before the right one

"Hard plates must not bend like cloth" has two failure modes and each gets an exact test in [`measure-plate-rigidity.mjs`](../../../../scripts/character-assets/measure-plate-rigidity.mjs).

Under **animation**, a mesh deforms because different vertices follow different bones. A piece whose every vertex carries one influence at full weight is transformed by a single bone matrix, so every internal distance survives every pose of every clip. That is settled by reading the weights, which covers the whole 57-clip library at once instead of hoping the sampled frames were the revealing ones.

Under the **fit**, the first rule emitted one translation for the whole piece. The render showed what the number did not: the pauldrons sank into the stout shoulder and all but vanished, because one offset vector cannot keep a cap-shaped plate clear of flesh inflating by up to 34% in every direction. The second rule fitted a single uniform scale and translation to the whole mesh, and came back with a 3.5% scale and a vertical shift — a left and a right pauldron move in opposite directions along X, so one transform can only express what they share. The third fits **one similarity per rigid piece**, grouped by primary joint, which is exact for this weighting.

| Piece | Vertices on one bone at full weight | Distinct bones | Slender scale | Stout scale | Worst departure from a uniform scale |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| WardenPauldrons (left) | 100% | 1 | 0.8596× | 1.1767× | **0.0001 mm** |
| WardenPauldrons (right) | 100% | 1 | 0.8628× | 1.1724× | **0.0001 mm** |
| WayfarerTunic (control) | 12.2% | 15 | 0.920× | 1.089× | 16.0–34.4 mm |
| WayfarerTrousers (control) | 14.7% | 6 | 0.953× | 1.060× | 24.4–28.8 mm |

Each pauldron is a pure similarity to float precision: the same plate at a different size, no angle changed, nothing bent. The cloth control has no such factor, which is what makes the plate result mean something. A uniform scale is not bending — it is what armour sizing is — and the two pauldrons are deliberately *not* rigid relative to each other, because the arms that carry them are not.

[Blender review renders](../../../../ve-capture/character-mmo/m005/plate/) show the plate over the three bodies, front and side. The first version of the prototype also showed the shoulder through the plate at an 18 mm standoff after two smoothing passes; it was rebuilt at 32 mm with one gentler pass.

## Live, in the real route

`?garmentFit=refit` points the streamed equipment pack at a generated manifest of the same eight catalogue items, with recomputed byte lengths and SHA-256 digests, so it goes through `equipment-stream.js`'s real verification rather than around it. [`check-garment-fit-live.mjs`](../../../../scripts/character-assets/check-garment-fit-live.mjs) ran four cases to full playable state at 1280×720 with seven enemies: stout with shipped garments, stout refitted, slender refitted, and stout at 1.15 height refitted.

| Case | Shaped meshes at boot | After equipping a further piece | Weights |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| stout, shipped | 1 (the body alone) | 1 | [0, 1] |
| stout, refit | 5 | 6 | [0, 1] |
| slender, refit | 5 | 6 | [1, 0] |
| stout at 2.02 m, refit | 5 | 6 | [0, 1] |

Every shaped mesh carried exactly the requested weights, at boot, after a late equip, and after a failed request — a garment arrives at weight 0, which is its shipped shape, so the weights are re-applied whenever an equipment request settles. A request for an unknown item failed and left the committed loadout and every weight untouched. Havok active, no page or console errors in any case. Results in [`live-garment-fit.json`](../../../baselines/character-mmo/m005/live-garment-fit.json).

## Live editing against a committed shape

Measured on the stout refitted outfit at 1280×720, 900 frames per run, alternating so drift shows as disagreement between the pairs. [`shape-preparation.json`](../../../baselines/character-mmo/m005/shape-preparation.json) has the raw rows.

| Run | Mean frame | p95 | Weight update |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Static (control) | 9.213 ms | 20.7 ms | — |
| Sweep, a new blend every frame | 9.241 ms | 20.7 ms | 0.0127 ms mean, 0.2 ms worst |
| Static (control) | 9.290 ms | 20.8 ms | — |
| Sweep | 9.280 ms | 20.5 ms | 0.0106 ms mean, 0.1 ms worst |

The two pairs straddle each other, so sweeping every frame is inside run-to-run noise: writing the weights for a whole outfit costs about **0.012 ms**. These are `requestAnimationFrame` intervals inside an instrumented probe, so the ~108 FPS here is a property of the measurement loop and is not comparable to the route benchmark; the static rows are the control.

The resident cost is **0.611 MiB** of morph deltas across five meshes — Lite stores 6 floats per target and vertex, position then normal — against **312.7 KiB** to re-upload and a median **0.3 ms** (worst 1.9 ms) of CPU to bake a committed shape over 13,340 vertices.

For one character, live morphing is cheap enough that committing buys nothing but the 0.611 MiB. The trade inverts with population: the resident cost is per distinct shaped mesh, so a crowd of varied shapes pays it per actor while a committed bake pays once. M009 owns that, with these two numbers as its inputs.

## Limits and inputs for M006

- **33 body vertices still leave their garment at the shape extremes**, all at hems. Adding material is authoring, not fitting.
- **Animation is not evaluated for garment fit.** The coverage measurement is the shared rest pose. A clean rest pose does not prove a clean walk cycle, and nothing here measures cloth against cloth.
- **The plate is a prototype.** Crude edges, asymmetric vertex counts, no normal map, no production silhouette. It proves the deformation rule, not the art.
- **The plate is not in the catalogue** and so is not reachable from the game's equipment path. Adding it would be a production catalogue change this milestone excludes.
- **The refitted pack is a developer candidate** under `.cache/`, reached only through an explicit query. The shipped garments are unchanged.
- **Layer precedence is untouched.** M005 refits each garment independently; it does not decide what happens where two garments overlap. M007 owns coverage and seam semantics.
- **Per-actor memory at crowd scale is unmeasured.** The 0.611 MiB figure is one character.
- **The girth field is implemented twice**, in `girth-field.mjs` and inline in `build-human-shape-family.mjs`. They agree today, and the body candidate's hash is the check that they still do.

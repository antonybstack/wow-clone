# M1 — Havok ownership and teardown on Lite 1.28.0

This checkpoint follows [the migration plan](babylon-lite-1.31.1-migration-plan.md) without changing the Lite or Havok versions. `src/player.js` now registers one idempotent physics cleanup callback before asynchronous Havok initialization. It tracks every returned caller-owned static mesh/box and animated collider body and shape, plus the camera query shape. The character controller remains outside that ledger because Lite owns its body, current capsule shape, and query collectors. This remains true after a height change replaces the capsule. Cleanup detaches the camera sweep, disposes the controller, releases the query shape, removes caller bodies, releases caller shapes, and calls public `disposePhysics(world)` while the world is valid. Setup failure and zero-collider fallback use the same path; a populated authored-collider set that fails to initialize now fails explicitly instead of silently using non-Havok walking. Late animated-collider registration and physics callbacks are guarded after disposal. The nearby source comment links Lite's [physics ownership contract](https://github.com/BabylonJS/Babylon-Lite/blob/npm-lite-v1.31.1/docs/lite/architecture/42-physics.md).

The [real-Havok lifecycle tests](../scripts/test-player-physics-lifecycle.mjs) cover controller replacement after a height change, two query collectors, camera shape, caller-owned aggregate body/shape, zero-collider disposal, partial registration failure, repeated cleanup, and late world creation. Together with camera-rig tests, **8/8 pass**. The ordinary Vite build passes. A fresh [built-runtime report](baselines/lite-1.28.0-m1-2026-09-26/built-runtime.json) for `ashenReach-BRaUbfmw.js` verified seven enemies, shadows, 1280×720 canvas, active Havok, **14.05 m** normal walking, no recovery teleport, and no runtime/GPU errors. The [camera report](baselines/lite-1.28.0-m1-2026-09-26/camera.json) and [west-chapel stairs report](baselines/lite-1.28.0-m1-2026-09-26/stairs.json) passed; stairs were entered and returned with zero recoveries. One first camera attempt missed its right-mouse orbit assertion; a fresh retry passed without a code change. The owned harness was stopped afterward.

I reviewed the [14.727-second live motion MP4](https://ve.sparkify.dev/wow-clone/ashen-reach/lite128-m1/2026-09-26-physics-motion.mp4) from the exact built candidate. Its [contact sheet](baselines/lite-1.28.0-m1-2026-09-26/contact.jpg), [capture report](baselines/lite-1.28.0-m1-2026-09-26/report.json), and [timestamp manifest](baselines/lite-1.28.0-m1-2026-09-26/capture-manifest.json) cover lamps/walking, equipment, hit/cast, camera obstruction, cathedral movement, and woodland. The video is H.264, 1280×720 square pixels, no rotation; VE returned `video/mp4` and byte-range playback, and direct Chromium playback advanced at the matching dimensions and 14.727 s duration.

`check-enemy-collision.mjs` passed in the clean baseline slot, but missed its head-on stop assertion in another slot with both untouched HEAD and M1 code: the controller slid laterally around the stationary enemy. The same-slot control excludes this as evidence of an M1-specific regression. The test is frame-pacing/solver-sensitive and remains a follow-up; movement behavior was not changed to satisfy a single run. A complete in-place game restart also remains outside this player-only lifecycle checkpoint because UI, input, audio, and HUD have separate owners.
